# Comparison between POCS Technology and Traditional Surgery

> **Technology Introduction** • January 5, 2025

---

## Key Takeaway

POCS (PerOral Cholangioscopy) represents the latest advancement in gallstone treatment. Unlike traditional open surgery or laparoscopic surgery, POCS is a "scarless" procedure performed through the mouth. It offers significant benefits including minimal trauma, faster recovery (often returning to normal activities within 3-7 days), and the ability to preserve the gallbladder and its function in suitable candidates.

## Introduction

Medical technology for treating gallstones has evolved significantly—from traditional open surgery to laparoscopic surgery, and now to the advanced POCS (PerOral Cholangioscopy) technique. This article provides a detailed comparison between POCS technology and traditional surgical methods to help patients understand the pros and cons of each treatment option.

## Comparison of Surgical Methods

### Traditional Open Surgery

This is the earliest method for treating gallstones. It requires a large 10–15 cm incision in the abdomen to remove the gallbladder or stones.

**Procedure**:
1.  General anesthesia.
2.  A large incision (10–15 cm) is made in the abdomen.
3.  The gallbladder is removed or stones are extracted under direct vision.
4.  The incision is sutured.

### Laparoscopic Surgery

A minimally invasive improvement over open surgery, this method uses several small holes.

**Procedure**:
1.  General anesthesia.
2.  3–4 small holes (0.5–1 cm) are made in the abdomen.
3.  A laparoscope and surgical instruments are inserted.
4.  Surgery is performed while viewing a monitor.
5.  Small incisions are sutured.

### POCS Technology (PerOral Cholangioscopy)

POCS is the most advanced minimally invasive method. A cholangioscope is passed through the mouth into the bile duct, requiring no external incisions.

**Procedure**:
1.  Local anesthesia or mild sedation.
2.  The scope passes through the mouth, esophagus, stomach, and duodenum.
3.  A thin cholangioscope provides direct visualization of the bile duct.
4.  Stones are fragmented and removed under direct vision.
5.  Procedure is completed with no incisions on the body surface.

## Advantage Analysis

### Advantages of POCS Technology

**1. Incision-Free and Scar-Free**
*   No abdominal incisions required.
*   No post-operative scars.
*   Excellent cosmetic outcome.

**2. Minimal Trauma, Fastest Recovery**
*   No opening of the abdomen; minimal disturbance to internal organs.
*   Patients can often walk the same day.
*   Discharge typically within 1–3 days.

**3. Preserves Gallbladder Function**
*   Stones can be cleared while keeping the gallbladder intact.
*   Maintains normal digestive function.
*   Avoids complications associated with gallbladder removal (cholecystectomy).

**4. Direct Visualization, Precision, and Safety**
*   The bile duct can be observed directly from the inside.
*   Procedures are performed under direct vision, avoiding "blind" risks.
*   More thorough stone removal.

**5. Suitable for Complex Cases**
*   Intrahepatic bile duct stones.
*   Bile duct strictures.
*   Recurrent stones after previous surgery.
*   Patients for whom traditional surgery is difficult or risky.

### Limitations of Traditional Surgery
*   Large trauma and slow recovery.
*   Necessity of gallbladder removal.
*   Noticeable post-operative scars.
*   Higher risk of complications.
*   Not suitable for elderly or frail patients.

## Suitability Comparison

### POCS is Best For:
*   Patients with common bile duct stones.
*   Patients with intrahepatic bile duct stones.
*   Patients who wish to preserve their gallbladder.
*   Elderly or frail patients unfit for major surgery.
*   Patients with recurrent stones after surgery.

### Laparoscopic Surgery is Best For:
*   Patients with gallbladder stones (where removal is necessary).
*   Patients with gallbladder polyps.
*   Patients with atrophic or non-functional gallbladders.

### Traditional Open Surgery is Best For:
*   Patients with a history of multiple abdominal surgeries.
*   Severe abdominal adhesions.
*   Need for simultaneous treatment of other abdominal conditions.
*   Complex biliary reconstruction surgery.
