## Key takeaway

During a cholecystitis attack, adopting the correct posture can significantly alleviate pain by reducing abdominal tension. The most recommended positions are the **left lateral decubitus position with knees bent** (fetal position) or the **semi-recumbent position** (upper body elevated at 30-45 degrees).

**Important:** Avoid lying flat on your back with legs extended, as this increases abdominal muscle tension and exacerbates pain. If pain persists for more than 6 hours despite position changes, or if accompanied by fever or jaundice, seek emergency medical attention immediately.

## Why Does Posture Matter?

The gallbladder is located in the right upper quadrant of the abdomen. When inflamed, the gallbladder wall becomes swollen and sensitive.

*   **Muscle Tension:** Lying flat with straight legs tightens the rectus abdominis muscles, compressing the inflamed gallbladder.
*   **Gravity:** Elevating the upper body or curling up utilizes gravity to shift abdominal organs away from the gallbladder area, reducing pressure.

## Recommended Postures

1.  **Left Lateral with Knees Flexed:** Lie on your left side, pulling your knees up towards your chest. This relaxes the abdominal wall and reduces direct pressure on the right upper quadrant.
2.  **Semi-Fowler's Position:** Prop yourself up at a 30-45 degree angle. This is often more comfortable for breathing and reduces localized pressure.

## What to Avoid

*   **Supine Position:** Lying flat on the back.
*   **Right Lateral Position:** Lying on the right side directly compresses the inflamed organ.

## Additional Care

*   **Fasting (NPO):** Stop eating and drinking immediately to stop gallbladder contraction.
*   **Ice Packs:** A cold compress on the right upper abdomen may help numb the pain (avoid heat packs in the acute phase).

## When to Seek Medical Help

Go to the ER if:
*   Pain lasts > 6 hours.
*   Fever develops.
*   Jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes) appears.
*   Severe vomiting or rigid abdomen occurs.
