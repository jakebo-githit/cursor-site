### Key Takeaways
- When acute cholecystitis strikes, immediately stop eating, especially fatty foods, to reduce the burden on the gallbladder.
- If the pain is unbearable, over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or acetaminophen can be used, but long-term use is not recommended.
- Seek immediate medical attention if you experience high fever, jaundice, or persistent vomiting.
- Acute cholecystitis usually requires surgical intervention, but whether to preserve the gallbladder depends on the specific situation.
- Early medical care can effectively prevent complications and improve treatment outcomes.

## What Are the Typical Symptoms of Acute Cholecystitis?
When acute cholecystitis occurs, patients typically experience severe pain in the right upper quadrant of the abdomen, which may radiate to the right shoulder or back. Other common symptoms include:
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fever, with a temperature exceeding 38°C
- Tenderness in the abdomen, especially the right upper quadrant
- Abdominal bloating or discomfort
- Loss of appetite

Conclusion: The typical symptoms of acute cholecystitis are severe right upper quadrant pain, accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and fever. If you experience these symptoms, take immediate action and consider seeking medical care.

### Misconception
Some people believe that they can continue eating, especially high-fat foods, to help relieve pain during an acute cholecystitis attack. This is incorrect. High-fat foods can exacerbate the burden on the gallbladder and intensify the pain.

## Can You Take Pain Relievers When You Have an Acute Cholecystitis Attack?
If the pain is unbearable during an acute cholecystitis attack, over-the-counter pain relievers such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen can be used. However, note the following:
- Use only for temporary pain relief; long-term use is not recommended
- If the pain does not subside or symptoms worsen after taking pain relievers, seek immediate medical attention
- Avoid aspirin, as it can increase the risk of bleeding
- Lie down and avoid strenuous activities while taking pain relievers
- Closely monitor any changes in symptoms after taking pain relievers

Conclusion: During an acute cholecystitis attack, you can take over-the-counter pain relievers for temporary pain relief, but long-term use is not recommended, and you should closely monitor any changes in symptoms.

### Misconception
Some people believe that pain relievers can completely cure cholecystitis. This is incorrect. Pain relievers can only temporarily alleviate pain and do not address the underlying cause of cholecystitis.

## Can Acute Cholecystitis Heal on Its Own? What Happens if It's Left Untreated?
Acute cholecystitis typically does not heal on its own and requires timely treatment. If left untreated, it can lead to serious complications such as:
- Gallbladder perforation, leading to peritonitis
- Gallbladder abscess formation
- Obstruction of the bile ducts due to impacted gallstones
- Spread of infection, causing systemic infection
- Loss of gallbladder function, affecting digestion

Conclusion: Acute cholecystitis usually does not heal on its own. If left untreated, it can result in severe complications like gallbladder perforation, abscess formation, and bile duct obstruction.

### Misconception
Some people believe that acute cholecystitis can be cured through dietary adjustments alone. While dietary changes can help manage symptoms, they cannot cure acute cholecystitis.

## How to Manage an Acute Cholecystitis Attack at Home?
When an acute cholecystitis attack occurs, you can take the following steps to manage it:
- Immediately stop eating, especially fatty foods, to reduce the burden on the gallbladder
- Lie down and avoid strenuous activities
- Take over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or acetaminophen for temporary pain relief
- Drink plenty of water to stay hydrated
- Apply a warm compress to the painful area to alleviate discomfort

Conclusion: When an acute cholecystitis attack occurs, immediately stop eating, lie down, take over-the-counter pain relievers, drink plenty of water, and apply a warm compress to the painful area.

### Misconception
Some people believe that massaging the painful area can help relieve pain during an acute cholecystitis attack. This is incorrect. Massaging can exacerbate inflammation and increase pain.

## Should the Gallbladder Be Preserved or Removed After an Acute Cholecystitis Attack?
Acute cholecystitis often requires surgical intervention. Whether to preserve the gallbladder depends on the specific situation:
- For patients experiencing their first attack with mild symptoms, conservative treatment with antibiotics may be an option
- For patients with recurrent attacks or severe symptoms, cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal) is recommended
- Patients who choose to preserve their gallbladder should have regular follow-ups to monitor gallbladder function
- After gallbladder removal, patients should adjust their diet to avoid high-fat foods
- Surgical treatment can effectively prevent recurrence of cholecystitis

Conclusion: Acute cholecystitis often requires surgical intervention, but whether to preserve the gallbladder depends on the patient's specific situation. Patients with their first mild attack can opt for conservative treatment, while those with recurrent or severe attacks should consider cholecystectomy.

### Misconception
Some people believe that gallbladder removal will affect digestive function. This is incorrect. Most patients can resume normal digestive function after surgery, requiring only dietary adjustments in the initial postoperative period.

## Risk Boundaries and Signals for Medical Attention
When acute cholecystitis occurs, seek immediate medical attention if you experience the following:
- Persistent pain that does not subside and gradually worsens
- Fever exceeding 38.5°C, accompanied by chills
- Jaundice, with yellowing of the skin and eyes
- Persistent vomiting, making it difficult to eat
- Significant tenderness in the abdomen, with abdominal muscle rigidity

Conclusion: If you experience persistent pain, a fever exceeding 38.5°C, jaundice, or persistent vomiting during an acute cholecystitis attack, seek immediate medical attention.

### Misconception
Some people believe that they do not need medical attention if the pain subsides. This is incorrect. Even if the pain lessens, there may still be underlying serious complications, and timely medical care is essential for safety.
