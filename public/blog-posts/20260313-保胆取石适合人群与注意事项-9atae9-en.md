Gallbladder stones are a common digestive system disorder, and the traditional treatment approach has typically been cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal). However, with advances in medical technology, gallbladder preservation surgery offers an alternative treatment option that preserves organ function for certain patients. So, which patients are suitable for gallbladder preservation surgery?

Gallbladder preservation surgery is a minimally invasive procedure that removes gallstones from the gallbladder while preserving the organ's function. This approach is particularly suitable for younger patients, those with good gallbladder function, and those with fewer stones. According to the Chinese Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Biliary Tract Diseases, the indications for gallbladder preservation surgery mainly include good gallbladder function, patent cystic duct, fewer stones, and no significant gallbladder wall thickening [1].

## Patient Groups Suitable for Gallbladder Preservation Surgery

1. **Younger Patients**: Patients under 50 years old generally have better gallbladder function, making it more likely for gallbladder function to recover postoperatively. Studies have shown that the gallbladder function preservation rate in young patients after gallbladder preservation surgery can exceed 80% [2].

2. **Patients with Good Gallbladder Function**: Those who demonstrate good gallbladder contractility (typically >30%) on ultrasound examination, with a patent cystic duct and no impacted stones in the gallbladder neck. These patients have a higher likelihood of gallbladder function recovery after surgery.

3. **Patients with Single or Multiple Small Stones**: Stones typically <2cm in diameter, with fewer stones (usually <5), and no significant gallbladder wall thickening. Research indicates that stone size and number are important factors affecting the success rate of gallbladder preservation surgery [3].

4. **Patients Without Significant Inflammatory Response**: Those without a history of acute cholecystitis attacks, normal gallbladder wall thickness (usually <3mm), and no perigallbladder adhesions. These patients have lower surgical risks and relatively lower postoperative complication rates.

5. **Patients With Special Needs**: Such as professionals requiring physical demands (pilots, athletes) or those who strongly wish to preserve their gallbladder, gallbladder preservation surgery can be considered if other indications are met.

## Situations Unsuitable for Gallbladder Preservation Surgery

1. **Severely Impaired Gallbladder Function**: Patients with gallbladder contractility <30% or obstructed cystic duct. Even after stone removal, gallbladder function recovery is unlikely, with higher stone recurrence rates postoperatively [4].

2. **Acute Cholecystitis Attack Period**: During acute inflammation, the gallbladder wall is congested and edematous, making surgery difficult and increasing complication risks.

3. **High Risk of Gallbladder Cancer**: Patients with significantly thickened gallbladder wall (>4mm), gallbladder polyps >1cm, or complicated with adenomyomatosis, who have a risk of cancer transformation, should undergo cholecystectomy.

4. **Combined Common Bile Duct Stones**: Patients with concomitant common bile duct stones require comprehensive treatment planning, as simple gallbladder preservation surgery may not address all issues.

## Practical Recommendations

- **Comprehensive Preoperative Evaluation**: Before gallbladder preservation surgery, thorough gallbladder function assessment is necessary, including ultrasound, cholecystography, and other examinations to ensure surgical indications are met.
- **Choose Reputable Hospitals**: Gallbladder preservation surgery requires advanced technical skills. It's advisable to select experienced hepatobiliary surgeons and qualified medical institutions for the procedure.
- **Understand Postoperative Recurrence Rates**: The stone recurrence rate after gallbladder preservation surgery is approximately 10-30%. Regular follow-up examinations are necessary, with retreatment if needed [5].
- **Adjust Lifestyle Habits**: Postoperatively, maintaining a healthy diet with low-fat, low-cholesterol foods is essential to reduce the risk of stone recurrence.
- **Regular Follow-up**: Regular check-ups at 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year post-surgery are necessary to evaluate gallbladder function and treatment effectiveness through ultrasound and other examinations.

## When to See a Doctor

1. Severe right upper abdominal pain, especially accompanied by fever, nausea, and vomiting, may indicate an acute cholecyst attack requiring immediate medical attention.
2. Persistent abdominal pain, fever, or jaundice after surgery may suggest complications and require prompt medical consultation.
3. If stone recurrence or significant decline in gallbladder function is detected during follow-up, consult a doctor about further treatment options.
4. Progressive worsening of digestive symptoms like indigestion and blo affecting normal daily life warrants medical evaluation.
5. Signs of infection such as wound redness, swelling, or discharge after surgery require immediate medical attention.
6. Any symptoms or discomfort inconsistent with expected surgical outcomes should be discussed with your doctor promptly.
