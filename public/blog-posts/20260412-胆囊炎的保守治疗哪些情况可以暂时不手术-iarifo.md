## 胆囊炎必须马上手术吗？

作为一名肝胆外科医生，我在门诊经常被问到：“医生，我得了胆囊炎，是不是必须马上手术？”事实上，虽然手术是根治急性胆囊炎的主要手段，但在很多情况下，保守治疗同样扮演着至关重要的角色。保守治疗不仅是手术前的桥梁，对于部分特定人群而言，更是最安全的治疗策略。

## 什么是保守治疗？

保守治疗，即非手术治疗，其核心目标是控制感染、缓解症状、维持生命体征平稳。基本的保守治疗通常包括以下几个方面：

1. **禁食与胃肠减压**：停止进食以减少胆囊收缩，让发炎的胆囊得到充分休息。
2. **静脉补液与营养支持**：维持水、电解质平衡，保证身体所需营养。
3. **抗感染治疗**：静脉使用广谱抗生素控制胆道系统的细菌感染。
4. **解痉止痛**：使用解痉药物缓解胆道痉挛，减轻患者剧烈疼痛。

## 哪些情况适合保守治疗？（核心适应症）

根据临床指南与研究，保守治疗主要适用于以下几种情况：

### 1. 症状较轻的轻度急性胆囊炎
对于局部体征轻微、全身炎症反应较轻（如低热、白细胞轻度升高）的患者，单纯依靠保守治疗往往能取得很好的效果。

### 2. 作为手术前的过渡准备
部分患者在急性发作期就诊时，胆囊周围组织水肿严重。此时立即进行腹腔镜胆囊切除术难度大，且容易造成胆管损伤。通过先期的保守治疗，待炎症消退后择期手术，安全性会大大提高。

### 3. 患者身体条件无法耐受手术
这是保守治疗最常见的强适应症。例如高龄且合并严重心肺疾病的患者、处于心肌梗死急性期的患者，或患有严重凝血功能障碍的人群。对于这类患者，手术风险远大于疾病本身的风险。

### 4. 患者个人意愿拒绝手术
在充分告知手术风险与保守治疗复发风险的前提下，尊重部分因个人原因强烈拒绝手术的患者的意愿。

## 保守治疗的局限性与注意事项

必须强调的是，保守治疗并不能彻底根除结石性胆囊炎的病因。如果在保守治疗期间，患者的症状没有缓解，甚至出现高热、黄疸加重、腹膜炎体征（提示胆囊坏死或穿孔），医生会立即改变策略，紧急采取手术或经皮肝胆囊穿刺引流（PTGBD）干预。

## Is Immediate Surgery Always Necessary for Cholecystitis?

As a hepatobiliary surgeon, I frequently encounter patients terrified by the prospect of immediate surgery after a diagnosis of acute cholecystitis. While surgical removal of the gallbladder (cholecystectomy) remains the definitive treatment, conservative, non-operative management plays a critical role. It serves not only as a bridge to safe surgery but also as the primary treatment strategy for specific patient populations.

## What Does Conservative Management Entail?

The primary goals of conservative treatment are to control the inflammatory process, alleviate symptoms, and stabilize the patient's vital signs. A standard protocol generally includes:

1. **Fasting (NPO) and Bowel Rest:** Withholding oral intake minimizes gallbladder contraction, allowing the inflamed organ to rest. In severe cases, a nasogastric tube may be placed.
2. **Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation:** Fasting necessitates IV fluids to correct dehydration and maintain electrolyte balance.
3. **Antibiotic Therapy:** Administering broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics is crucial to treat and prevent biliary sepsis.
4. **Analgesics and Antispasmodics:** Pain control is essential. Antispasmodic drugs help relieve biliary colic caused by smooth muscle spasms.

## Key Indications for Conservative Treatment

According to current medical guidelines, conservative management is most appropriate in the following clinical scenarios:

### 1. Mild Acute Calculous Cholecystitis
Patients presenting with mild localized abdominal pain, low-grade fever, and normal or slightly elevated white blood cell counts often respond exceptionally well to conservative measures. The inflammation can often be resolved without immediate surgical intervention.

### 2. A Bridge to Delayed Surgery
Performing surgery during the acute, severe phase of cholecystitis can be technically demanding due to severe tissue edema. This increases the risk of complications, such as bile duct injury. Conservative management allows the acute inflammation to subside, making a planned, interval laparoscopic cholecystectomy much safer.

### 3. High Surgical Risk and Medical Comorbidities
This is perhaps the strongest indication for non-operative management. Surgery may be deferred or avoided entirely if the patient is a poor candidate due to advanced age combined with severe cardiopulmonary disease, an acute concurrent medical emergency (like a recent heart attack), or severe, uncorrectable coagulopathy. For these high-risk patients, percutaneous transhepatic gallbladder drainage (PTGBD) combined with antibiotics is often a life-saving alternative.

### 4. Patient Refusal of Surgery
After thorough counseling regarding the risks of recurrent cholecystitis and potential complications, a patient's autonomous decision to decline surgery must be respected. In such cases, conservative management becomes the default approach.

## Limitations and When to Change Course

It is imperative to understand that conservative treatment does not cure the underlying cause. Once the acute episode resolves, the risk of recurrence remains high. Furthermore, conservative management requires close monitoring. If the patient's condition deteriorates—indicated by persistent high fever, worsening abdominal pain, or the development of jaundice—the medical team must immediately pivot to surgical intervention or emergency drainage to prevent life-threatening complications like gallbladder perforation.
