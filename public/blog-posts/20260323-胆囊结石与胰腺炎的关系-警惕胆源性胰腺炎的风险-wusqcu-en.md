# The Relationship Between Gallstones and Pancreatitis: Understanding Biliary Pancreatitis Risk

> **Gallstones** • March 23, 2026

## Understanding Biliary Pancreatitis

Biliary pancreatitis refers to acute pancreatitis caused by biliary tract diseases, primarily gallstones. In Asian populations, approximately 50-70% of acute pancreatitis cases are gallstone-related, making it the most common etiology.

## Pathogenesis

Two main theories explain how gallstones trigger pancreatitis:

**Bile Reflux Theory**: Microscopic stones or biliary sludge pass from the gallbladder and become impacted at the ampulla of Vater, the common channel of the biliary and pancreatic ducts. Bile salts and lysolecithin damage the pancreatic ductal barrier, activating trypsinogen and initiating pancreatic autodigestion.

**Obstruction Theory**: Stones block the Oddi sphincter, preventing pancreatic juice outflow. This increases intraductal pressure, rupturing pancreatic acini and allowing enzymes to infiltrate the pancreatic parenchyma, triggering an inflammatory cascade.

## Risk Factors

Gallstone patients with these characteristics face higher pancreatitis risk:
- Stone diameter less than 5mm (microliths pass more easily through the cystic duct)
- Wide cystic duct (>3mm)
- Multiple small stones
- Biliary sludge presence
- Post-high-fat meals

## Clinical Presentation

Typical symptoms of biliary pancreatitis include:
- Sudden, severe epigastric or left upper quadrant pain, radiating to the back
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fever
- Jaundice in some patients
- History of previous biliary colic

## Diagnostic Approach

Diagnosis requires combined evaluation:
1. **Serum amylase and lipase**: Typically elevated >3 times normal
2. **Liver function tests**: Elevated ALT, AST, ALP, and bilirubin suggest biliary origin
3. **Abdominal ultrasound**: Detects gallstones and common bile duct dilation
4. **CT or MRI**: Assesses pancreatic inflammation severity and complications
5. **MRCP**: Visualizes biliary anatomy and stone location

## Treatment Strategies

**Acute Phase Management**:
- NPO status with nasogastric decompression if needed
- Aggressive intravenous hydration
- Analgesia
- Proton pump inhibitors
- ICU monitoring for severe cases

**Biliary Intervention**:
- Mild pancreatitis: Cholecystectomy during the same admission is recommended
- With choledocholithiasis: ERCP with stone extraction
- Severe pancreatitis: Delayed surgery after stabilization

## Prevention

For patients with symptomatic gallstones, particularly those with microliths or multiple stones, elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy is the most effective method to prevent recurrent biliary pancreatitis. Early intervention before complications develop significantly reduces morbidity and mortality associated with this condition.
