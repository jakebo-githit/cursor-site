After gallbladder preservation surgery, do you know how to manage your condition scientifically to prevent stone recurrence?

Gallbladder preservation surgery (cholecystolithotomy) is a minimally invasive procedure that preserves gallbladder function for eligible patients with gallstones. Postoperative follow-up and long-term management are crucial for preventing stone recurrence and ensuring successful outcomes.

## Practical Takeaways

• **Regular Follow-up**: Abdominal ultrasound examinations should be performed at 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year after surgery, then annually, to monitor gallbladder function and detect any recurrence.

• **Dietary Adjustments**: Maintain a low-fat diet, avoiding fried foods, fatty meats, and animal organs. Increase fiber intake and maintain regular meal patterns to support digestive health.

• **Medication for Prevention**: As prescribed, take medications like ursodeoxycholic acid to help dissolve residual tiny stones and prevent new stone formation.

• **Lifestyle Modifications**: Maintain a healthy weight, avoid rapid weight loss, engage in appropriate exercise, quit smoking, limit alcohol consumption, and reduce risk factors for stone formation.

• **Symptom Monitoring**: Pay attention to any abdominal pain, indigestion, or other symptoms, documenting them to report to your healthcare provider.

## When to See a Doctor

• Experience persistent right upper quadrant abdominal pain that worsens in intensity or duration
• Develop jaundice (yellowing of skin or eyes), dark urine, or pale stools
• Experience nausea, vomiting, or fever, which may indicate recurrent cholecystitis
• Ultrasound examination reveals new stones or significant thickening of the gallbladder wall
• Experience worsening indigestion symptoms that don't respond to dietary adjustments
