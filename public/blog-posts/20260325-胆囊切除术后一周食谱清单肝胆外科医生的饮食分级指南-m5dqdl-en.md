## Executive Summary (30-Second Read)

The core principles for the first week after gallbladder removal are **low fat, small frequent meals, and gradual progression**. Without the gallbladder's storage function, bile flows continuously into the intestine, temporarily reducing fat digestion capacity. During the first 7 days, limit daily fat intake to **20-30 grams**, spread across 5-6 meals, prioritizing easily digestible proteins and complex carbohydrates. About 80% of patients can gradually return to a normal diet within 2-4 weeks, but proper dietary management in the first week directly impacts recovery speed and complication risk.

---

## Postoperative Days 1-2: Clear Liquid Phase

During early recovery, intestinal function hasn't fully returned. Start with clear liquids and progress gradually.

**Recommended Foods:**
- Rice water, thin lotus root starch (100-150ml every 2-3 hours)
- Skinned chicken or vegetable broth
- Diluted apple juice (1:1 with warm water)

**Avoid:** Milk, soy milk, sugary juices, any greasy foods.

> **Clinical Note:** Progress to the next phase only after passing gas and having a bowel movement. If significant bloating or nausea occurs, extend the clear liquid phase by 1-2 days.

---

## Postoperative Days 3-4: Low-Fat Semi-Liquid Phase

This phase introduces soft, low-fat semi-solid foods.

**Sample Daily Menu:**

| Meal | Food |
|------|------|
| Breakfast | Millet porridge + Steamed egg custard |
| Snack | Steamed peeled apple puree (100g) |
| Lunch | Soft noodles + Minced chicken breast + Tender greens |
| Snack | Half a banana |
| Dinner | Yam rice porridge + Small portion of steamed fish |

**Cooking Tips:** All meats should be trimmed of fat and connective tissue. Vegetables must be cooked soft. No frying or stir-frying.

## Postoperative Days 5-7: Low-Fat Soft Diet Phase

This critical recovery phase allows more variety, but fat control remains essential.

### What Carbohydrates Are Safe to Eat?

**Recommendations:**
- White rice, millet porridge, yam porridge, soft noodles
- Steamed buns, white toast (crusts removed)
- Mashed potatoes (no butter)

**Common Misconception:** Not all whole grains are beneficial immediately after surgery. High-fiber grains (brown rice, oats, corn) may burden the digestive system. Introduce gradually after 2 weeks.

### How to Safely Consume Protein?

**Recommendations:**
- Chicken breast, turkey (skinless, fat-free)
- White fish (cod, sea bass, flounder)
- Egg whites, tofu, silken tofu
- Skim milk (if tolerated)

**Daily Target:** 60-80 grams of protein, distributed across meals.

**Common Misconception:** Rich bone broth is nutritious. Actually, white cloudy broths are high in fat and purines, which can hinder recovery. Choose fat-skinned clear broths instead.

### Which Fruits and Vegetables Are Appropriate?

**Recommended Vegetables:** Tender greens, winter melon, loofah, peeled cucumber, yam, pumpkin, carrots (all cooked soft).

**Recommended Fruits:** Bananas, ripe papaya, steamed apples, cantaloupe.

**Common Misconception:** Raw fruits are more nutritious. In early recovery, steamed or pureed fruits reduce digestive irritation.

### When Should You Adjust Your Diet?

**Adjustment Signals:**
- Post-meal diarrhea more than 3 times/day → Reduce fat to 15g/day, extend semi-liquid phase
- Noticeable bloating → Reduce portion size, increase meal frequency
- Fatty stools (floating, greasy) → Strictly limit fat, consider bile salt supplementation

## Warning Signs and When to Seek Medical Care

**Emergency Signals (Seek care within 24 hours):**
- Severe, persistent abdominal pain, especially in the upper right quadrant
- Fever above 38.5°C (101.3°F)
- Jaundice (yellowing of skin or eyes)
- Intractable vomiting, unable to eat
- Blood in stool or black, tarry stools

**Schedule a Follow-up If:**
- Diarrhea persists beyond 5 days
- Weight loss exceeds 3 kg in one week
- Persistent loss of appetite

Maintain communication with your surgeon during recovery. For long-term dietary management, consult with a registered dietitian.
