## Key takeaway

**Patients with gallstones can eat fruits, and moderate consumption is beneficial for gallbladder health.** The key is choosing the right types, controlling portions, and eating them properly. Apples, pears, strawberries, and blueberries are good choices, while high-fat fruits like durian and coconut meat should be limited.

## Can Gallstone Patients Eat Fruits?

Yes, most fruits are safe for gallstone patients. Fruits are generally low in fat and rich in vitamins, minerals, and dietary fiber, which can support gallbladder function and overall metabolism.

The main considerations are:
- **Fat content**: Most fresh fruits contain less than 0.5% fat, making them unlikely to trigger gallbladder contractions
- **Sugar content**: Excessive fructose may affect lipid metabolism, so moderation is key
- **Fiber content**: Soluble fiber like pectin can help regulate bile metabolism

## Recommended Fruits for Gallstone Patients

**Best choices (high fiber, low sugar):**
- Apples (rich in pectin, eat with skin after washing)
- Pears (high water content, gentle on gallbladder)
- Berries (strawberries, blueberries, raspberries - rich in antioxidants)

**Moderate consumption:**
- Bananas (choose slightly underripe for lower sugar)
- Grapes (limit to 100-150g per day)
- Citrus fruits (oranges, grapefruits - rich in vitamin C)

**Fruits to limit or avoid:**
- Durian (high fat content, about 5g per 100g)
- Coconut meat (very high saturated fat, about 33g per 100g)
- Overripe tropical fruits (mango, jackfruit - very high sugar)

## How to Eat Fruits Safely

- **Timing**: Best between meals (mid-morning or mid-afternoon); avoid on empty stomach or right after meals
- **Portion**: 200-300g total per day, about one fist-sized portion at a time
- **Form**: Whole fruits are better than juice (juice lacks fiber and concentrates sugar)
- **Temperature**: Avoid very cold fruits; let refrigerated fruits reach room temperature first

## When to Seek Medical Attention

Seek **immediate** medical care if you experience:
- Severe, persistent abdominal pain (especially lasting more than 30 minutes)
- Fever and chills (may indicate acute cholecystitis)
- Jaundice (yellowing of skin or eyes, indicating bile duct obstruction)
- Persistent nausea and vomiting

Schedule a consultation if you have:
- Repeated discomfort after eating certain fruits
- Gallstones growing in size or number on follow-up ultrasound
