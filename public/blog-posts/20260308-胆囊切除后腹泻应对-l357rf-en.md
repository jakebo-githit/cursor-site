After gallbladder removal surgery (cholecystectomy), many patients experience diarrhea and indigestion. This occurs because the liver continuously produces bile that no longer gets stored and concentrated in the gallbladder, directly entering the intestine instead. This constant flow of bile can overwhelm the digestive system, leading to changes in bowel habits.

## Understanding Post-Cholecystectomy Diarrhea

The medical term for this condition is postcholecystectomy syndrome, with diarrhea being one of the most common symptoms. Studies suggest that up to 20-40% of patients may experience some form of digestive disturbance after gallbladder removal, with diarrhea being particularly prevalent.

The underlying mechanisms include:
- Bile acid malabsorption due to continuous bile flow
- Altered gastrointestinal motility
- Changes in gut microbiota composition
- Fat malabsorption in some cases

## Practical Management Strategies

• **Adopt a Low-Fat Diet**: Reduce fat intake to 30-40 grams per day. Avoid fried foods, fatty meats, full-fat dairy products, and rich sauces. Opt for lean proteins like skinless poultry, fish, and plant-based proteins.

• **Eat Smaller, More Frequent Meals**: Instead of three large meals, try 5-6 smaller meals throughout the day. This approach helps your digestive system handle bile more effectively and prevents overwhelming your intestines.

• **Increase Soluble Fiber**: Incorporate foods like oats, apples, bananas, carrots, and psyllium husk. Soluble fiber helps absorb excess bile and adds bulk to stool, reducing diarrhea frequency.

• **Avoid Trigger Foods**: Identify and eliminate foods that worsen your symptoms. Common triggers include caffeine, alcohol, spicy foods, artificial sweeteners, and high-fat dairy products.

• **Stay Hydrated**: Diarrhea can lead to dehydration. Drink plenty of water throughout the day and consider electrolyte solutions if symptoms are severe.

## When to See a Doctor

• If diarrhea persists for more than a week without improvement
• If you experience signs of dehydration such as dry mouth, decreased urination, or dizziness
• If you notice blood in your stool or black, tarry stools
• If you develop severe abdominal pain or fever
• If you experience unexplained weight loss
