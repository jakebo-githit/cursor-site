## Key Takeaways (30-second read)
When experiencing an acute gallbladder attack, it is crucial to stop eating, especially fatty foods, and seek medical attention promptly. Home emergency measures include resting, applying heat, and drinking moderate amounts of water. Treatment options depend on the severity of the condition and the patient's specific circumstances, whether it be gallbladder preservation or removal.

## What are the typical symptoms of an acute gallbladder attack?
**Conclusion:** During an acute gallbladder attack, patients often experience pain in the upper right abdomen, nausea, and vomiting.
- **Right Upper Abdominal Pain:** The pain is usually continuous and may worsen intermittently, radiating to the right shoulder or back.
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** These symptoms often accompany the abdominal pain.
- **Fever:** Some patients may have a low-grade fever.
- **Jaundice:** Severe cases may present with jaundice, characterized by yellowing of the skin and eyes.
- **Abdominal Distension:** Patients may feel bloated.
**Common Misconception:** Some people believe that drinking a lot of water can relieve symptoms during an acute gallbladder attack, but excessive water intake can burden the gastrointestinal tract and worsen the condition.

## Can you take pain medication when you have a gallbladder attack?
**Conclusion:** It is not recommended to take pain medication on your own during an acute gallbladder attack.
- **Masking Symptoms:** Pain medication can reduce pain but may also mask the severity of the condition, leading to delayed treatment.
- **Side Effects:** Certain pain medications can exacerbate gastrointestinal symptoms and cause other complications.
- **Avoid Medication Before Medical Evaluation:** Do not use any medication before a proper diagnosis is made.
**Common Misconception:** Some people think that pain medication can immediately relieve pain and avoid the need for medical attention, which is very dangerous.

## Can acute cholecystitis resolve on its own?
**Conclusion:** Acute cholecystitis typically does not resolve on its own and requires timely treatment.
- **Progressing Inflammation:** If left untreated, the inflammation can continue to develop, leading to serious complications such as gallbladder perforation.
- **Worsening Pain:** The inflammation can cause the pain to worsen, affecting quality of life.
- **Systemic Symptoms:** The inflammation may cause fever, jaundice, and other systemic symptoms.
- **Professional Evaluation:** Timely medical attention allows for a professional evaluation and the development of an appropriate treatment plan.
**Common Misconception:** Some people believe that acute cholecystitis can resolve with rest and dietary adjustments, which is incorrect.

## How should you manage an acute gallbladder attack at home?
**Conclusion:** When experiencing an acute gallbladder attack, home emergency measures include resting, applying heat, and drinking moderate amounts of water.
- **Stop Eating Immediately:** Especially avoid fatty foods to prevent further strain on the gallbladder.
- **Rest:** Lie down and minimize physical activity.
- **Apply Heat:** Use a hot water bottle or warm towel on the upper right abdomen to help relieve pain.
- **Moderate Water Intake:** Maintain a moderate water intake to avoid dehydration.
- **Monitor Symptoms:** Closely monitor any changes in symptoms and seek medical attention if they worsen.
**Common Misconception:** Some people believe that drinking a lot of water is beneficial during an acute gallbladder attack, but excessive water intake can burden the gastrointestinal tract.

## Should you preserve or remove your gallbladder after an attack?
**Conclusion:** The treatment choice after a gallbladder attack depends on the severity of the condition and the patient's specific circumstances.
- **Gallbladder Preservation:** Suitable for patients with mild inflammation and good gallbladder function.
- **Gallbladder Removal:** Recommended for patients with severe inflammation, loss of gallbladder function, or recurrent attacks.
- **Medication:** Mild inflammation can be managed with medication.
- **Individualized Treatment:** Treatment plans should be individualized and determined by a professional doctor.
- **Long-term Management:** Regardless of the choice between preservation and removal, long-term management is necessary to prevent recurrence.
**Common Misconception:** Some people believe that preserving the gallbladder is always better than removing it, but gallbladder removal can be safer and more effective in certain situations.

## When should you seek urgent care?
**Conclusion:** If you experience any of the following symptoms during an acute gallbladder attack, seek medical attention immediately.
- **Severe Pain:** Unbearable pain that continues to worsen.
- **High Fever:** Body temperature exceeding 38.5°C.
- **Jaundice:** Yellowing of the skin and eyes.
- **Persistent Vomiting:** Frequent vomiting that prevents eating.
- **Peritonitis Symptoms:** Such as muscle tension and abdominal tenderness.
- **Shock Symptoms:** Such as a drop in blood pressure and an increased heart rate.
**Common Misconception:** Some people believe that only severe pain requires medical attention, but other symptoms are equally important and should not be ignored.
