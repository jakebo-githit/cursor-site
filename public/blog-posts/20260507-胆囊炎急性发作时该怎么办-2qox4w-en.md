## Key Takeaway
When experiencing an acute cholecystitis attack, patients should immediately stop eating, especially high-fat foods, and take pain medication to alleviate symptoms. However, they should not rely on pain medication long-term. If symptoms such as high fever, jaundice, or persistent severe pain occur, immediate medical attention is required. Treatment options for cholecystitis include medical and surgical interventions, and the choice depends on the severity and doctor's recommendation.

## Identifying Typical Symptoms of Acute Cholecystitis
Acute cholecystitis is an acute inflammation of the gallbladder, commonly presenting with symptoms such as right upper abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and fever. The pain typically worsens after eating, especially after consuming high-fat foods. It may radiate to the right shoulder or back and is often accompanied by bloating and discomfort. If any of the following symptoms occur, seek medical attention immediately:
- High fever (temperature exceeding 101.3°F)
- Jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes)
- Persistent severe pain
- Significant abdominal tenderness

### Can I Take Pain Medication During an Acute Cholecystitis Attack?
**Conclusion: Yes, you can take pain medication, but do not rely on it long-term.**
- Pain medication can provide temporary relief but does not address the underlying cause of cholecystitis.
- Common pain relievers include ibuprofen and acetaminophen.
- Follow medical advice and avoid overuse.
- **Misconception: Pain medication can cure cholecystitis.**

### What Should I Eat During an Acute Cholecystitis Attack?
**Conclusion: Stop eating immediately, especially high-fat foods.**
- High-fat foods can stimulate gallbladder contraction, exacerbating pain.
- Drink water in moderation, but avoid sugary drinks and coffee.
- Maintain a light diet, avoiding greasy and spicy foods.
- **Misconception: You can continue your regular diet.**

### Should I Go to the Hospital Immediately During an Acute Cholecystitis Attack?
**Conclusion: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience high fever, jaundice, or persistent severe pain.**
- Acute cholecystitis can lead to complications such as gallbladder perforation or abscess, which can be life-threatening.
- Early medical attention helps in timely diagnosis and treatment, reducing the risk of complications.
- Home emergency measures can provide temporary relief but cannot replace professional medical care.
- **Misconception: Home emergency measures can fully cure cholecystitis.**

### What Are the Treatment Options for Acute Cholecystitis?
**Conclusion: Treatment options include medical and surgical interventions, and the choice depends on the severity and doctor's recommendation.**
- Medical treatment: Primarily used for mild to moderate acute cholecystitis, involving antibiotics and pain medication.
- Surgical treatment: Recommended for patients with recurrent attacks or severe complications, such as cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal).
- Conservative treatment: For patients who are not suitable for surgery, gallbladder preservation can be attempted but requires close monitoring.
- **Misconception: All patients with cholecystitis need surgical treatment.**

## Risk Boundaries and Signs to Seek Medical Attention
- **High fever**: Temperature exceeding 101.3°F, indicating a worsening infection.
- **Jaundice**: Yellowing of the skin and eyes, suggesting bile duct obstruction.
- **Persistent severe pain**: Pain that does not subside, indicating potential gallbladder perforation or abscess.
- **Significant abdominal tenderness**: Increased tenderness in the abdomen, indicating the spread of inflammation.
